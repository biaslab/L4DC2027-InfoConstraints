# Writing a scientific paper

Apply these rules to every sentence written for a paper. The audience is the control systems community.

## Voice and tone
- Write like a scientist. No sales pitches or vague ideas.
- Use technical jargon appropriate for a control systems audience.
- Zero hedging. "We show", not "We believe". "X reduces Y by 13×", not "X may help reduce Y".
- No filler adjectives: never use "novel", "significant", "state-of-the-art", "comprehensive", "robust", "substantial", "promising" or "impressive". Replace them with specific numbers or delete them.
- No exclamation marks. No rhetorical questions outside introductions.

## Sentences
- Mean sentence length is about 25 words. Maximum is about 40 words, and only in contribution lists.
- Connect sentences with appropriate conjunctive adverbs.
- No unnecessary auxiliary sentences or clauses, such as ", and the price it costs."
- Avoid overusing colon and semicolon sentence structures.
- Use em-dashes sparingly.

## General patterns to apply throughout
- Give each sentence a stated subject and a finite verb. Do not delete "this", "the design" or "it", and do not turn objects into subjects ("The count enters only through the order…"). Naming the actor restores flow.
- Convert noun-heavy sentences into two sentences. A sentence that carries three claims joined by "and" or commas should be split.
- Replace elliptical juxtaposition ("X is decided by Y. Z is decided by W.") with an explicit contrast where it aids comprehension. Keep the punch line, but add one connective.
- Structure the abstract as claim, then mechanism, then benefit.

## Paragraphs
- Paragraphs have 4–6 sentences.
- Every paragraph does exactly one of the following: make a claim, present evidence, or synthesise a takeaway.
- Topic sentences assert claims. Never open a paragraph with background or context.

## Content
- Interpret figures, don't just cite them. "Figure 3 shows that X, confirming Y", not "See Figure 3".
- Justify every design choice immediately. Not "we use X", but "we use X because Y".

## Before finishing
Reread the new or edited text against each rule above. Pay particular attention to passive constructions, sentences without a stated subject, sentences carrying several claims, paragraph openers, headings, sentence length and filler adjectives, because these violations are the easiest to miss.
