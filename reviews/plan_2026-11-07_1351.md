## What the checks so far established

- **The ρ = 2 vs γ = 1 puzzle (W8) is a solver artefact.**
  - The dual MPC cost equals 2F with ρ = 2γ (`agents/dual_mpc/dual_mpc.py`).
  - At ρ = 2 with S_* = σ², the caution and probing terms cancel to second order around rest: (1+x) − ln(1+x) = 1 + x²/2 + …, with x = P u²/σ².
  - The dual MPC starts at rest at b0 = 0. However, IPOPT, started from the ±0.5 symmetry-breaking plan, returns u ≈ −8·10⁻⁹ at t = 2–3. That shifts the gain estimate to −1.6·10⁻⁷, and the inputs grow to O(0.1) within six steps (seeds 1000 and 1001).
  - The message planner returns exact zeros, so EFE stays at rest (RMSE 0.48 and 0.25 against 0.125 and 0.121 for the dual MPC on the same seeds).
  - The b0 = 0 advantage of the "tuned dual MPC" in Fig. 3a therefore comes from round-off, and EFE's turn-off sits on a degenerate boundary.
- **The integrator's best probing weight depends on the prior** (tuning seeds 1000–1049, RMSE):

  | b0 | best ρ | runner-up |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0 | 10 (0.120) | 2 (0.136) |
  | 1 | 0 (0.120) | 10 (0.126); 50 gives 0.148 |
  | 4 | 0–2 (0.1003) | 50 gives 0.137 |

  No single ρ is best for every b0. This is the clean demonstration that the goal-set multiplier needs, and it is not yet in the paper.
- **V-A contains a factual error.** "Within 0.006 of them for b0 ≥ 0.5" is wrong: at b0 = 0.5, CBFE reaches 0.1313 and the dual MPC 0.1245.
- **On the integrator, the constraint matters only at b0 = 0.** For b0 ≥ 0.5, EFE averages 0.114, the same as CBFE.

## P1 — The claims at stake (W1–W3)

**P1.1 Sensitivity to γ_max on the arm** (W1)
- **Run:** CBFE with the KKT multiplier for γ_max ∈ {5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500}, under no change and under unknown polarity, on the 10 test seeds.
- **Report:** RMSE, NLL, activity per round, and the share of steps at γ_max.
- **Cost:** about 1 h CPU (480 steps per episode at 40–90 ms per step), about 10 min on 8 cores.
- **Decides the framing:**
  - flat for γ_max ≳ 20 → "insensitive to its cap";
  - otherwise γ_max is the tuned parameter on the arm, and the abstract must say so.

**P1.2 Probing-weight curves under both conditions** (W2; the key figure for "tuning-free")
- **Arm:**
  - Run the dual MPC with ρ ∈ {0, 2, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500} and MPC-X with accuracy ∈ {1e-4, 4e-4, 1e-3, 3e-3}, under known and under unknown polarity, on the held-out seeds (100–105) and the test seeds.
  - Tune like-for-like on held-out seeds with unknown polarity, and report both tunings.
- **Integrator:** after P0.1, run the ρ grid per b0 on the test seeds.
- **Figure:** RMSE against the probing weight, one curve per condition or prior, with CBFE as a horizontal band. If the best ρ differs between conditions while one CBFE setting stays near the lower envelope, the tuning-free claim holds.
- **Cost:** arm about 2 h CPU (about 15 min on 8 cores); integrator about 10 min.

**P1.3 Sweep of the goal tolerance** (W3)
- **Integrator:** S_* ∈ {σ²/16, σ²/4, σ², 4σ², 16σ²}.
- **Arm:** goal standard deviations × {0.5, 1, 2, 4}.
- **Report:** the share of active steps, the share saturated at γ_max, RMSE, and EFE.
- **Expected:**
  - the multiplier is interior for S_* ≥ R and saturates for S_* < R, by the reviewer's feasibility bound;
  - EFE probes robustly for S_* > σ²;
  - S_* = σ² is the degenerate boundary.
- **Cost:** integrator about 30 min; arm about 1 h CPU.

**P1.4 Decision: a noise-aware floor** (W1, W3; changes the method)
- **Idea:** replace S_* by S_* + R_t in eq. (10). The floor then never exceeds the attainable one-step information, so the multiplier is always zero or an interior root, and γ_max drops out.
- **Interpretation:** demand information while the parameter-induced spread exceeds the goal tolerance on top of the irreducible noise.
- **Test:** both examples, next to the current floor.
- **Paper:** the information-floor paragraph and eq. (10); Lemma 4.5 is unchanged.
- **When:** run after P1.1–P1.3 show whether saturation costs performance.

**P2.3 Computational baselines** (W6)
- **Optimisers:**
  - L-BFGS with the exact adjoint gradient;
  - Newton on the joint objective, with a Gauss–Newton Hessian from the output Jacobian and a dense Cholesky factorisation;
  - Nesterov-accelerated gradient descent.
- **Settings:**
  - the integrator, for K ≤ 100;
  - the arm, for K ∈ {5, 10, 20, 50}, where n_u = 2, n_y = 4 and the features are nonlinear;
  - a full CBFE call broken down into planning, root finding and the floor grid.
- **Expected:** Newton matches the sweep count, and L-BFGS lies in between. The claim becomes "message passing gives Newton-type steps at a cost linear in K". The dense-factorisation sentence of V-C goes, or is quantified.
- **Cost:** about 1 h.

**P2.4 Tuning-free exploration baselines** (W7)
- **Posterior-sampling (Thompson) MPC:** sample the coefficients from the MNW posterior every step and plan certainty-equivalently with the same planner (γ = 0, zero coefficient covariance). Implement it in the experiment layer, because agents may not import each other.
- **Certainty-equivalence MPC:** the dual MPC without caution, at ρ = 0.
- Run both on both examples.
- **Cost:** about half a day of code; runs about 1 h.

**P2.5 Integrator DP with |u| ≤ 1** (W5; optional)
- The bound breaks the normalisation, so V depends on (η, β, log P), a 3-D dynamic program: about 1.5 h on 24 cores.
- Otherwise keep the unbounded DP as a stated lower bound.
