## 3. Integrator: steps

1. **Score in G, not RMSE.** benchmark.pkl stores y, u, b̂ and P per episode, so G needs no rerun. The goal term is ½y²/σ², because S* = σ². The prediction term uses μ = y_prev + b̂u and Σ = σ² + Pu². Dynamic programming minimises the expected goal term, so G also makes the DP reference exact, which the mean of per-episode RMSEs is not.

6. **Draft.** The numbers below are the current RMSE ones. Replace them after step 1.

```latex
\paragraph{Integrator with an unknown gain}
The benchmark of \citet{aastrom1986dual} has the state $x_k = x_{k-1} + b\, u_k + w_k$ with $w_k \sim \cN(0, \sigma^2)$ and $\sigma = 0.1$, the output $y_k = x_k$ and the unknown gain $b \sim \cN(b_0, 1)$. The controllers regulate the output to $y_* = 0$ within $S_* = \sigma^2$ over $T = 50$ steps, and a Kalman filter tracks the gain. Under $b_0 = 0$, the unknown sign of the gain turns no-info-CBFE off, because every input only spreads the prediction. Over 200 episodes for each $b_0 \in \{0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4\}$, info-CBFE attains an RMSE of 0.116, against 0.114 for the dual MPC with the tuned $\rho = 5$ and 0.116 for MPC-X, whereas dynamic programming attains 0.104 without an input bound. The good probing weights form a narrow window, because outside $5 \leq \rho \leq 10$ the RMSE of the dual MPC exceeds that of info-CBFE by at least 7\%, and for $\rho \leq 2$ it never moves under $b_0 = 0$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:quad}(d)). Info-CBFE needs no such search. Its constraint acts on 2.0\% of the steps, and its active multipliers have a median of 3.2, which corresponds to $\rho = 6.4$ inside the window.
```

7. **Abstract, contributions and Conclusion** name both systems. Draft abstract (X is a placeholder):

```latex
Explicit dual controllers add a probing term to a predictive control cost, and its weight must be tuned per system and task. We derive this weight from the goal instead. We cast receding-horizon dual control as Bethe free energy minimization on the factor graph of a planning model, constrained by a floor on the information that the next output carries about the unknown parameters and states. The floor compares the predictive covariance of goal-reaching inputs with the goal tolerance, so the KKT multiplier of the constraint acts as a probing weight that vanishes once the uncertainty fits within the tolerance. On the integrator benchmark of {\AA}str{\"o}m and Helmersson, the untuned controller comes within X\% of a dual MPC whose weight is tuned within a narrow window. On a quadcopter with an unknown compass fault, it matches a dual MPC tuned at the nominal sensor noise and lowers the learning cost by 22\% against MPC with experiment design.
```
