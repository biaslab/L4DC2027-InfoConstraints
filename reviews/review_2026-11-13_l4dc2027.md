# Review: "Goal-derived information constraints for dual control" (L4DC 2027)

*Simulated L4DC 2027 review of `submission/main.pdf` (12 pp., 10 pp. of content) and `submission/supplementary.pdf` (14 pp.), 2026-11-13. This version supersedes the review of the earlier draft.*

## 1. Summary

The paper casts receding-horizon dual control as constrained minimisation of the Bethe free energy of a Forney-style factor graph of the planning model. Form-constraining the parameter and current-state beliefs to the filter posteriors removes the optimism of control-as-inference; an information-gain constraint between the next output and (x_{t+1}, θ) then yields a control posterior whose exponent holds the tracking and caution terms plus γ times the information gain (Thm. 5). The novelty lies in the floor: β_t is an excess entropy that compares the goal-weighted predictive covariance with R + S_* (Eq. 11), so the KKT multiplier vanishes as soon as the parameter-induced spread fits the goal tolerance (Lemma 6). The method is evaluated on a simulated quadcopter with an unknown compass rotation and wind; the untuned controller matches a dual MPC tuned at the nominal noise and beats MPC-X, with a mechanistic account (first commanded tilt 7° → 29°, 26 % of later steps active). Proofs, the message equations, a solver comparison, an integrator benchmark with a DP reference and a light–dark task are in the supplement.

## 3. Strengths

- **The derivation is clean and I believe it.** The Bethe-collapse accounting (S1.1) is correct: under q_a = f_a ∏ q_i the node terms become negative input entropies, and orienting each edge into exactly one consumer cancels them against the edge entropies. Thm. 5 follows, and Lemma 6 is a correct exponential-family argument: h′ = Var ≥ 0, strict by the shared null sets of prior and posterior, and the Laplace/essential-supremum step is handled properly, including the fact that q* puts no mass where p(u) puts none.
- **The floor (11) is a genuinely good idea.** It converts "how much information?" into "how much predictive spread does the goal tolerate?", which is the right question, and it gives an automatic off-switch that neither a tuned ρ nor MPC-X's κ has. The comparison of the three ways of setting the weight is the conceptual core of the paper.
- **The new §4.3 and S2.3 answer the right question.** The message equations (20), the quadratic cost-to-go (S27), the control message (S28) and the coordinate objective (S29) make the method reproducible, and the solver study in S2.3 (same 38 % of active steps, multipliers within 3 %, first inputs within 10⁻⁴ on 77 % of steps, G = 3838 vs 3897) is exactly the kind of check a reader wants. This is a real improvement.
- **Experimental hygiene.** All controllers share model, filter and horizon; the ablation differs only in γ; seeds are paired and the intervals bootstrapped; the design search and its contamination of the test seeds are disclosed; code and every hyperparameter are given. The prior moments are right — I checked E[cos χ] = sin 2.6/2.6 = 0.198, sd(cos χ) = 0.61, sd(sin χ) = 0.76.
- **The mechanism analysis is more convincing than the means.** The 7° vs 29° first tilt, the 1092 [453, 1823] gap accrued in the first 4 s, and the diagnosis of MPC-X (requirement binds on 0.07 % of steps, worst decile +25 %) explain *why* the numbers differ.
- **Honest limitations.** Greedy first-node constraints, open-loop stability, consistency after switch-off and the saturation regime are all stated.

## 4. Major concerns

**M1. The design was selected with the test seeds (S2.5).** This is still the largest threat to the empirical claim, because the claim is precisely "untuned method vs tuned baselines". The final setting (priors matched to the fault distribution, the random-walk W, the fault range) was chosen after seeing seeds 1000–1099. I would want (i) results on a fresh, untouched seed set (e.g. 4000–4099) with all numbers replaced by those, and (ii) a statement of how much of the 635 [267, 1046] gap to MPC-X and the 442 [−410, 1427] gap to the dual MPC survives. The 22 %/35 % learning-cost figures inherit the issue. Disclosure is commendable, but it does not remove the bias.

**M2. "No probing weight to tune" moves the tuning into the prior.** The paper says the prior on (a, b) must match the fault moments "because only a prior that states the uncertainty lets the floor see it", and S2.5 reports that a prior which trusts the compass failed on the test seeds. So the method has no ρ, but it does have the prior spread, the random-walk W, the floor cap I_max − 0.01, the 4096 Monte-Carlo nodes and the dual bracket. Only the baselines get a tuning grid; none of these is analysed. A sensitivity study of at least the prior spread and the cap is needed. Conceptually: the floor is only as trustworthy as the belief it evaluates, and an overconfident prior silently disables probing — the very failure the method is designed to prevent. This belongs in the main text.

**M3. The delta relative to [Kouw, 2026] is still only implicit.** The introduction now frames active inference as "largely limited to discrete-state discrete-action planning (van de Laar et al., 2022; Kouw, 2026)", which hints at the contribution, but the contribution paragraph does not say what is new relative to that paper, which already contains the goal-augmented planning graph, the Bethe collapse, an information constraint whose floor is derived from the goal, the stationary policy p(u) exp(goal cross-entropy + γ × MI), and the inactive/interior/saturated dual solve. The new elements are the continuous excess-entropy floor (11), the conditional-information treatment for continuous states, Lemma 6, and the quadcopter. One explicit sentence would pre-empt an incrementalism objection.

**M4. One experiment in the reviewed paper, and it is simulated.** The CFP states that decisions are based only on the 10-page paper, yet the conclusion reports the light–dark results (G = 2.27 vs 2.27 for MPC-X vs 2.02 for the dual MPC; 5.8 % vs 2.2 % of steps in the light) as if they were in the paper. Either compress the integrator study into the main text — it is cheap, has a DP reference and is the strongest evidence for "matches tuned dual MPC without tuning" — or drop the light–dark sentence from the conclusion. A second fault family (mass/thrust gain, or a deliberately misspecified prior) would also make the generality claim credible.

**M5. The reported flights do not use message passing, although the abstract says so.** The abstract states that the functional "is minimized through message passing", and §4.3 describes the sweeps, but S2.3 reports that "the flights of Section 5 compute the same fixed point with L-BFGS-B, because the coordinate updates converge slowly": a plan takes a median of 600 sweeps, a step a median of 2.7 s and up to 62 s, versus 271 ms with L-BFGS-B. Message passing gives G = 3838 instead of 3897 (−59 [−125, −4]). A reader of the 10-page paper cannot know which solver produced which number. State in §5 that the reported flights use L-BFGS-B, give the message-passing cost in the main text, and qualify the abstract. Relatedly, "the experiments find the same fixed point" overstates S2.3, where the first inputs agree to 10⁻⁴ on 77 % of steps and differ on the rest; say "closely track" or give the agreement rate.

**M6. Online cost.** 271 ms/step against 29–75 ms, and 0.63 s median (2.98 s max) on active steps against a 0.5 s control period: the controller misses the deadline in 76 % of active steps, and the message-passing variant is ten times slower. This should be stated up front, and a cheaper dual update suggested (e.g. warm-starting γ and evaluating one plan per iteration, or a sensitivity-based Newton step on h).

**M7. The submitted PDFs contain dangling cross-references.** These are mechanical but a reviewer will notice them:
- §4.3 cites "Section S3.3", which no longer exists; the material is in S2.3.
- §5 cites "Section S3.5"; the protocol is now S2.5.
- Appendix A says the node contributions are listed in "Table S1", but Table S1 is now the planning-model table; the node-contribution table was removed from S1.1.
- S1.1 refers twice to "the marginalization constraints (??)" because the label `eq:local` was removed from the main text when (5) was inlined.
- S1.2 contains the vacuous "With p(y_m | ϕ_*) = p(y_m | ϕ_*)" left over from replacing p_*.

## 5. Minor comments

1. Eq. (7): conditional mutual information needs q(a₂ | a₃) in the denominator, not q(a₂), and the definition should integrate over a₃. Harmless in this model (θ ⊥ u), but it is not the general definition.
2. Eq. (11) is called an "excess entropy", but Σ̄ is an average of covariances and H[N(0, Σ̄)] is the entropy at the averaged covariance, not the average entropy. The log-determinant ratio on the right is unambiguous; say which is meant.
3. §5, Baselines: "differs from info-CBFE only in how it weighs the information" — the dual MPC also probes at every k ∈ τ, whereas Thm. 5 sets γ_k = 0 for k > t+1. It differs in *where* as well as *how*; if the comparison is deliberately conservative for info-CBFE, say so.
4. Abstract: "on par with optimally tuned state-of-the-art dual controllers" (plural) — the paper matches the tuned dual MPC but beats MPC-X. Also "an information gain constraint between future outputs and the beliefs" (plural): by Thm. 5 the constraint is on the next output only, as §6 acknowledges.
5. Intro, contributions: "whose floors come from ... (Definition 3)" — Definition 3 only states the constraint; the floor is Eq. (11).
6. Fig. 2 legend says the dual MPC is tuned at ρ = 3·10³, but the text says the held-out seeds select ρ = 10⁴ at σ_f = 3. Clarify that panel (b) retunes per noise level.
7. Eq. (2): the prediction term contributes < 0.1 % of G and is identical across controllers (shared filter), so the claim that controllers are evaluated on prediction error is not operative. Drop it or justify it.
8. Naming: the supplement uses "CBFE" where the paper uses "info-CBFE" (S3.1, S3.2, Figs S1–S2).
9. Fig. 2(a) plots the running contribution (1/T)Σ_{k≤t}; at 4 s it is not G. Label it as such.
10. Typos and wording: "but that also depends tuning" (Intro, §1); "Most explicit dual controllers consist of a model predictive control (MPC) cost" (article); "the log-normaliser" vs "log-normalizer"; S1.1 "as Section 4 uses" is fine but "Section 4" could be "§4.2".
11. §6 leaves the consistency of the estimates after the constraint switches off open, but the paper does not say what is known about excitation in that regime. Even a short argument (or a pointer to a goal-relative excitation notion) would strengthen the discussion.

## 6. Questions to the authors

1. Fresh-seed results: how much of the gap to MPC-X and to the dual MPC survives on seeds never used for design?
2. Sensitivity to the prior spread, the floor cap and the number of Monte-Carlo nodes; and what happens with a deliberately overconfident prior — does the controller quietly stop probing?
3. Which solver produced the reported numbers, and can the message-passing sweeps be accelerated (fewer sweeps, Anderson acceleration, or a Gauss–Newton sweep) to make the analysed algorithm competitive with L-BFGS-B?
4. Is the applied input the mode of q*(u) or of the plan, and how sensitive is behaviour to that choice?
5. Does the method still probe when the true fault lies outside the prior support?
