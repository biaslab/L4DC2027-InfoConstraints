## Review

### Strengths

- **The derivation is compact and correct.** Lemma 4, Theorem 5 and Lemma 6 derive the tracking, caution and probing terms of explicit dual control from one Lagrangian.
- **The floor switches probing off by construction, and the experiments show it.** The constraint is active on 2.7% of the light–dark steps. On the quadcopter, it is active in the first two steps of every flight and on 26% of the later steps.
- **The quadcopter has a clear mechanism.** The trace term holds the first tilt of the cautious controller at 7°. MPC loses in the tail, where its worst 10% of flights cost 25% more, and on large faults, where CBFE wins 78% of the flights with |χ| > 2 rad.
- **The protocol is sound.** It uses shared seeds, models and filters, held-out tuning and paired bootstrap intervals.
- **The numbers check out.** Every quadcopter and light–dark number in Section 5 matches the result files, except possibly the tilt angles (W9).

### Weaknesses, most important first

**W1. The abstract, the contributions and the Conclusion claim results the paper no longer contains.**
- **The tight/loose claim is stale.** All three claim that one untuned configuration "costs less than every fixed weight over a tight and a loose goal". Section 5.2 no longer has two goals, and the new results contradict the claim: at the nominal light, the tuned dual MPC beats CBFE by 0.26 [0.12, 0.37] and wins 80% of the episodes.
- **The light–dark text and figure omit MPC-X.** MPC-X is tuned once, at the nominal light. It ties or beats CBFE at every light level (goal cost, MPC-X vs CBFE):

  | √r_L | MPC-X | CBFE |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0.01 | 1.42 | 1.54 |
  | 0.03 | 1.78 | 1.78 |
  | 0.1 | 1.91 | 2.79 |
  | 0.2 | 2.22 | 4.39 |
  | 0.3 | 3.16 | 5.82 |

  The caption of Fig. 3 ("degrades gracefully") and the text compare CBFE mainly with the dual MPC.
- **The quadcopter is missing.** The only main-text system with unknown parameters appears in none of the three.
- **The solver claim is wrong for one experiment.** The abstract says that message passing minimises the constrained free energy. The quadcopter planner, however, minimises J by L-BFGS-B with CasADi gradients (agents/cbfe_quad/agent.py). Only the light–dark planner runs sweeps.

**W2. The main text never tests the headline claim on a system with unknown parameters.**
- **The quadcopter does not test the headline.** The headline says that the multiplier removes a weight that designers tune. The quadcopter compares CBFE only with the cautious controller and with MPC on point estimates, so it shows that the constraint helps, not that it replaces a tuned weight.
- **The Section 5 opener describes no experiment.** Its first sentence ("matches dual MPCs that are tuned for each system and goal") applies to nothing in Section 5.1.
- **The only tuned comparison is mixed.** In the light–dark task, CBFE ties MPC-X and loses to the tuned weight at the tuning condition.
- **The missing baseline would make a strong point if the result holds.** The active quadcopter multipliers have a median of 5.9·10⁴ and a 90th percentile of 9.9·10⁴, and they reach the cap of 10⁶ in 0.1% of the cases. No designer would guess a weight of that size, and the multipliers are interior roots, not saturated ones.

**W3. The quadcopter tolerance does not describe the task.**
- **No flight reaches the tolerance.** The text asks the quadcopter to reach the target "within S_* = (0.03 m)² I over T = 20 steps". No flight of any controller ends within 3 cm:
  - the final miss has a median of 0.64 m for the oracle and 0.85 m for CBFE;
  - the smallest miss of any flight is 7.6 cm.
- **The position fix is coarser than the tolerance.** It has a standard deviation of 0.3 m.
- **The approach dominates the goal cost.** CBFE's goal cost of 3894 per step equals an RMS position error of 2.65 m, against 1.71 m for the oracle.
- **The tolerance acts only through the floor.** The search introduced the tight goal so that the constraint binds. A reviewer who asks for the final error will read S_* as a tuning knob.

**W4. The text only partly discloses the configuration search.**
- **The disclosure is incomplete.** The text says that a pilot on 30 separate seeds fixed the fault range and the sensor levels. In fact, results/search_log.jsonl logs 72 configurations on those seeds. One earlier candidate (±115°, compass prior) passed on one seed set and did not replicate on another.
- **The low-noise end of Fig. 2(b) favours the cautious controller.** It costs 276 [−943, 320] less than CBFE at σ_f = 0.2 and 180 [−828, 389] less at 0.5. The text says that these intervals include zero, which is fair.

**W5. The paper does not cite its direct precursor.**
- **The precursor already has the core idea.** kouw2026expected (IWAI 2026, in references.bib) imposes an information constraint on the Bethe Lagrangian and solves its KKT multiplier from a floor. It also describes the inactive, interior and saturated regimes. main.tex cites neither this paper nor kouw2024information.
- **The delta must be explicit.** It consists of:
  - continuous states and parameters;
  - the floor as excess predictive entropy over the goal tolerance, instead of the entropy of the goal prior;
  - the dual-control positioning;
  - the comparisons with dual MPC and MPC-X.
- **L4DC readers will expect the reinforcement-learning analogues.** Soft actor–critic sets its entropy temperature as the multiplier of an entropy floor (Haarnoja et al., "Soft actor-critic algorithms and applications", 2018). REPS bounds its policy update by a KL constraint (peters2010relative, in the bib). In both, the bound is a hyperparameter, whereas the floor here follows from the goal, which makes it the point of contrast.

**W6. The paper no longer describes the implementation, and the theory does not cover it.**
- **The planner description is gone.** Lines 292–296 are commented out. The paper no longer says that the control beliefs are modes, what J is, which solver each experiment uses, or how the plans start.
- **Lemma 6 does not cover the planner.** The lemma treats the Gibbs belief with the downstream beliefs fixed, whereas the planner applies the mode and replans all inputs for every trial multiplier. Line 279 states that the information gain is nondecreasing in γ:
  - for the global mode of the plan, a two-line exchange argument on F(u) + γ I(u₁) proves this;
  - for the local optimum that the planner finds, it can fail.
- **The control prior is never stated.** It is Beta(2, 2) per input, and the floor depends on it through p̃.
- **The grid claim holds for one experiment only.** "The floor averages over a grid of 399 inputs" is true for the light–dark task. The quadcopter uses 4096 uniform points in [−1, 1]³.
- **The cap is unreported.** Line 277 says the floor is capped "if necessary". The cap is I^max − 0.01 nats, and no agent stores how often it binds.

**W7. A careful reader will flag four definitions.**
- **Eq. (9) is not a conditional mutual information.** It divides by q(a₁ | a₃) q(a₂). Since x_{t+1} depends on u_{t+1}, the conditional mutual information needs q(a₂ | a₃). The proof of Theorem 5 (linearity in q(u_{t+1})) and the code both use the conditional form.
- **Definition 2 conflicts with line 192.** Definition 2 pins a node belief to its factor, and line 192 pins "the beliefs of the transition and measurement nodes to their factors". A conditional density is not a joint belief, and Definition 1 already fixes these nodes.
- **The Problem Statement does not cover Section 5.2.**
  - It asks how to gather information "about θ" and regulates the output, whereas Section 5.2 has no θ and a goal on the hidden state.
  - The light–dark floor ½ ln(A_t/S_*) also drops the R of Eq. (12).
  - Generalise the statement to a goal on a performance output, and present both floors as instances of one rule.
- **Eq. (14) needs parentheses** around the summand.

**W8. The evaluation ignores the tolerance.**
- **G is invariant to the scale of S_*.** Multiplying S_* by c divides G by c for every policy, so the controller that is optimal for G ignores the tolerance, whereas the floor uses it. A reviewer can therefore call S_* the tuning parameter of CBFE. One sentence in the Discussion pre-empts this objection, and a tolerance sweep answers it (Tier 2).
- **The joint constraint can mask the parameters.** State information alone can meet I[y : x, θ | u] when the sensors are precise, so CBFE may never probe θ. The chain rule I[y : θ | u] + I[y : x | θ, u] gives the fix, and one sentence suffices.

**W9. Section 5 contains stale facts.**
- **The horizon is wrong for the quadcopter.** Line 302 says that the predictive controllers share K = 5, whereas the quadcopter uses K = 6.
- **The tuning criterion is wrong.** Line 304 says ρ and the accuracy are tuned "by the mean RMSE". The light–dark tuning uses the mean goal cost (run_baselines.py), and the quadcopter has no tuned baseline.
- **ρ = 1 is unjustified, and the window would be stronger.** On the held-out seeds:
  - ρ ≤ 30 never visits the light and costs 4.24;
  - ρ = 100 costs 1.56;
  - ρ = 300 costs 7.75.

  The window of good weights is thus a single grid point, and reporting it argues better than the curve for ρ = 1.
- **The tilt angles may use another definition.** At the first step, the median angle of the thrust axis is 31.9° for CBFE, 32.0° for MPC and 6.8° for the cautious controller. The text says 29°, 31° and 7°. If CBFE and MPC tilt alike at the first step, the mechanism paragraph should locate their difference in the later steps.

**W10. Several symbols have two meanings.**
- k is both the time index and the drag coefficient in Eq. (20): "c_k − k ṗ_{k−1}".
- g is the measurement map in Eq. (1) and gravity in Eq. (20).
- γ is the KKT multiplier and the accuracy of MPC-X (Section 5.2 and the legend of Fig. 3).
- χ is the heading fault and the χ² quantile.
- ℓ is the width of the light and the leverage of the supplement.

**W11. The figures need work.**
- **Fig. 2 lacks subfigure labels**, which causes four of the undefined references.
- **Panels (d) of both figures carry little information.** They plot the wall clock against the share of active steps, which shows only that the root search costs time. Meanwhile, the headline mechanism, a multiplier that switches off, has no figure in the main text. Better candidates are γ_t or the share of active flights per step, or the ρ sensitivity of E1.
- **Fig. 2(a) hides the claimed difference.** Its log–log axes make the three curves almost coincide, although the text claims that CBFE closes in faster during the first 5 s.
- **Fig. 1 numbers messages that the text never uses.** It numbers ③–⑳, but the text defines only ① and ②.
- **Green means two controllers.** It marks MPC in Fig. 2 and MPC-X in Fig. 3.

**W12. Minor issues.**
- The title word "Localized" appears nowhere in the paper, so either define it (the constraint acts on one node) or drop it.
- The spelling mixes US forms (factorization ×8, marginalization ×5, minimized, Localized) with UK forms (minimises ×5, minimisation ×2, normalised, localises, favour).
- Five sentences exceed 40 words: line 282 ("Message passing computes …"), the sentence after Eq. (20), line 290 ("A plan alternates …"), line 329 ("Rotor drag grows …") and line 356 ("We compare CBFE …").
- Three phrases are passive or lack a subject: "This Lagrangian is to be minimized …", "which is solved from the KKT condition" and "In words, the approximate posterior is …".
- Nothing supports "Unlike bipartite factor graphs, FFGs make sequential computations explicit".
- The `\claude` note at line 327 names faessler2018differential, but the bib key is faessler2017differential. The entries leishman2014quadrotors, groves2013principles and abbasi2011improved are not in references.bib.
