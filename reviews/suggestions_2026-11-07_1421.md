# Suggestions for soundness and clarity

Written 2026-11-07, 14:21, in response to `review_2026-11-07_1336.md`. W1–W9 refer to its weaknesses. This file covers the method and the text; `plan_2026-11-07_1351.md` covers the experiments. Each item gives the issue, the fix and its cost.

## 1. Soundness of the method

**S1. Make the floor consistent with the one-step information** (W1, W3)
- **Issue:**
  - Eq. (10) compares the parameter-induced covariance with S_*, whereas the information (8) is measured relative to the noise.
  - The floor therefore exceeds the attainable information whenever |R S_*⁻¹| > 1. On the arm, 99.8% of the active multipliers sit at γ_max, which then acts as a fixed probing weight.
  - On the integrator, every multiplier is interior only because S_* = σ² lies exactly on the boundary.
- **Fix:**

      β_t = H[N(0, R_t + Σ̄_t)] − H[N(0, R_t + S_*)] = ½ ln|(R_t + Σ̄_t)(R_t + S_*)⁻¹|,   Σ̄_t = E_p̃[Σ^θ_{t+1}(u)].

  - *Meaning:* the information that the next measurement must deliver to shrink the predictive covariance at the goal-reaching inputs from R + Σ̄ to R + S_*. This reduces the parameter-induced spread to the goal tolerance. The floor and I_a then share units: both are log-ratios of predictive covariances that include the noise.
  - *Same switch:* β_t ≤ 0 exactly when R + Σ̄ does not exceed R + S_* in volume. For one output this is Σ̄ ≤ S_*, the switch of the current floor, so the switch-off story is unchanged.
  - *Always attainable when Σ^θ(u) = ℓ(u) R* (both examples). Then β_t = (n_y/2) ln(1 + ℓ̄*) − ½ ln|I + S_* R⁻¹| < (n_y/2) ln(1 + ℓ_max) = I^max. Hence case 3 of Lemma 4.5 cannot occur and γ_max leaves the method. The root can still be large when the goal-reaching inputs sit at the leverage maximum.
  - *Checked numerically* on 200 000 random scalar cases: the new floor never reaches I^max, whereas the current one does in 41 000 of them. The switch-off condition is identical.
  - *Integrator at b0 = 0* (P = 1, Beta(2,2) prior): 1.18 nats, against 1.50 for the current floor and I^max = 2.31. It is lower but still positive.
  - *Caveat:* for general multi-output models, log det is concave, so the average covariance Σ̄ can carry more information than any single input. Attainability therefore needs Σ^θ(u) proportional to a fixed matrix; state this.
  - *Against P1.4 of the plan* (S_* → S_* + R): P1.4 switches off only once Σ̄ falls below S_* + R, which changes the semantics. S1 keeps the switch at Σ̄ = S_*.
- **Cost:** eq. (10), (20), (24) and the floor paragraph; one line in `Planner.information_floor`; rerun both examples. The arm result may change, because the multiplier no longer saturates.

**S2. One constraint, on the node of the next input** (W3 last bullet, W4-ii)
- **Issue:**
  - Def. 4.2 imposes β_t on every parameterised node of the horizon. Since q(θ) does not learn within the plan, the plan must gather β_t K times.
  - The planner instead shares one multiplier among all nodes and solves it from the first node. That is a KKT point of neither problem.
  - It also breaks the premise of the proof of Lemma 4.5, because the downstream messages then depend on γ.
- **Fix:**
  - Constrain only the node a_{t+1} of the next input. Justification: in receding horizon, only the next measurement arrives before the next plan, and q(θ) is fixed within the plan.
  - Lemma 4.5 then holds exactly, and the root finding of Sec. IV-D is its KKT condition.
  - In code, apply γ only to the first slice (`probing_weight` for k = 0, zero otherwise, in `_slice_cost` and `_linearise`).
  - The per-node multipliers of Sec. VI become the extension.
- **Fallback:** keep the shared multiplier, but call it a heuristic (one weight for every information term, set by the constraint of the first step) and drop the KKT language for k > 1.
- **Cost:** Def. 4.2, the sentence after (13), Sec. IV-D and Sec. VI; a small code change; rerun, batched with P0.1.

**S3. Root finding with mode beliefs** (W4-i)
- **Issue:** the planner applies the mode û(γ) and solves I_b(û(γ)) = β_t. Lemma 4.5 (continuity, uniqueness) concerns E_{q*}[I_b] and does not cover this equation.
- **Fix:** add a remark.
  - For a global maximiser, I_b(û(γ)) is nondecreasing in γ. Adding the two optimality inequalities at γ₁ < γ₂ gives (γ₂ − γ₁)(I(û₂) − I(û₁)) ≥ 0.
  - The function can jump, so define γ_t as the smallest γ with I_b(û(γ)) ≥ β_t. Regula falsi brackets it, and the code already returns the feasible end at a jump.
  - The argument needs the rest of the objective to be independent of γ, which S2 provides.
- **Cost:** three sentences in Sec. IV-D.

**S4. State the assumptions of the results** (review Sec. IV-B/C, Appendix)
- **Lemma 4.3:**
  - Close every dangling edge (the last state, the last parameter copy) with a unit factor. Otherwise "every edge is the input of exactly one node" fails, and their entropies do not cancel.
  - State that the conditional factors are normalised.
- **Thm. 4.4:**
  - Assume q(θ) fixed and the graph without the parameter edges a tree. Every edge belief, and hence 𝓛, is then multilinear in the control beliefs. Move this from Sec. IV-D into the theorem.
  - Write out the induction from the goal nodes back to u_k in the proof.
- **Lemma 4.5:**
  - Assume a compact 𝒰 and a bounded I_b, which give integrability of p μ₀ e^{γI} and mutual absolute continuity.
  - Case 3 becomes "the constraint is infeasible"; the truncation at γ_max moves to Sec. IV-D. Under S1, case 3 cannot occur in the examples.
- **Lemma 5.1, proof:** centre y_k by M_tᵀφ_k before scaling by (1 + ℓ_t)^{-1/2}.
- **Sec. V-B, dropping c_t:** the floor (24) measures the coefficient-induced spread, so the matching left side is the coefficient information. The term c_t, the information about W, does not depend on the input and cannot be raised by probing.

**S5. Qualify what γ = 0 is** (W4, last bullet)
- The form constraints make θ and the predicted state independent inputs of every node. The caution of step k therefore counts only the spread that step k adds: Σ_j P u_j² for the integrator, instead of the exact open-loop P(Σ_j u_j)².
- Replace "the cautious open-loop controller of Section II" by "a cautious controller that penalises the parameter-induced spread of every step separately".
- Say in Sec. III that the equality nodes on θ close loops that Def. 4.1 cuts.

**S6. Scope of the model class** (review Sec. II and IV-A)
- **Belief nodes:** p(x_t|D_t) and p(θ|D_t) are separate factors. They give the joint filtering posterior only when the state is measured, as in both examples; otherwise they are a mean-field approximation. Qualify "This class contains state-space models…".
- **Rank-deficient Σ̄:** this happens when the parameters reach only some outputs within one step, e.g., position and velocity outputs with an unknown input gain. Then (10) gives β_t = −∞.
  - Evaluate the floor on the range of Σ̄.
  - Under S1 the log-determinant stays finite, but the unreached outputs would lower β, so restrict to the range there too.
- **Dependence on p(u):** say that the goal-reaching inputs are the inputs that the control prior admits and the mean model maps into the goal. With a flat mean model (b̂ = 0), the floor uses the spread of the prior, so p(u) sets it at b0 = 0. Name p(u) (see K3).

## 2. Soundness of the claims

**C1. The b0 = 0 comparison on the integrator is not sound before P0.1**
- The dual MPC escapes the symmetric rest point only through IPOPT round-off (plan, first finding).
- At S_* = σ², the caution and information terms at γ = 1 (ρ = 2) cancel to second order at rest, so EFE's turn-off sits on a degenerate boundary.
- In the text, state that rest is a stationary point of every objective that is symmetric in the sign of the gain, and how every planner treats it.
- **Drift experiment:** the known-start comparisons are unaffected, because b̂ is never exactly 0, and with the same input cost the dual MPC and CBFE agree to within 0.01. The dual MPC curve in the stationary-start panels of `fig_drift_loss.pdf` is this round-off escape.

**C2. "So they differ only in how they gather information" (Sec. V) is false at present**
- CBFE uses the Beta(2,2) prior, whose negative log-density −ln(1 − u²) is a barrier at the limits. The dual MPC and MPC-X use a quadratic input cost.
- On the drifting integrator, this difference alone costs CBFE 0.10–0.12 loss at ρ = 0.05–0.1. With the same input cost, the dual MPC at ρ = 2 and CBFE at γ = 1 give the same loss (paired difference ≤ 0.01).
- **Fix:** run the baselines with the Beta input cost (`DualMPCConfig(input_cost="beta")`, already implemented), or state the difference and its effect. In both cases, give the dual MPC cost as an equation with "ρ = 2γ up to the input cost" (W8).
- This is not in the plan; I suggest adding it as P0.3.

**C3. Recalibrate the claims to the evidence** (W1, W2, W5, W6). Final numbers follow the reruns; the wording issues are:
- **Abstract and contribution 3:** keep "tuned with a known polarity", and mention that the dual MPC at ρ = 2γ_max matches CBFE on the arm. "Without a tuned parameter" holds only where the multiplier is interior: everywhere under S1, otherwise on the integrator only.
- **V-A:**
  - "Within 0.006 of them for b0 ≥ 0.5" is wrong: at b0 = 0.5, 0.1313 against 0.1245.
  - After "Every active multiplier is an interior root", add "which S_* = σ² guarantees" (or delete under S1).
  - Report "within 14%" per prior mean: +23% at b0 = 0. Say that the DP has no input bound and minimises E Σ y², not the RMSE.
- **V-B:** "The multiplier supplies the probing weight" holds only while it is interior. Under saturation, the cap is the weight; say so, or adopt S1.
- **V-C and contribution 4:**
  - Say "than first-order gradient descent". The advantage is Gauss–Newton curvature at a cost linear in K, as in iLQR and DDP, not message passing as such.
  - Delete the dense-factorisation sentence or quantify it: a 100 × 100 Cholesky factorisation takes about 0.1 ms.
  - Define the speed-up as the median of per-problem ratios, and say that the study fixes γ = 1.
- **Conclusion:** "outperformed them without a tuned parameter" contradicts the ρ = 2γ_max result.

**C4. Uncertainty.** Report paired 95% intervals, since the seeds are shared (P2.1), and state which differences are significant. Several integrator differences are of the size of their standard error.

## 3. Clarity

**K1. Positioning** (W7). Add three sentences to Sec. I:
- **Against application-oriented experiment design and MPC-X:**
  - the tolerance of the requirement is the goal prior S_*, which the controller needs anyway, instead of a separate accuracy;
  - one variational problem yields tracking, caution, probing and the multiplier;
  - the KKT multiplier is set at every step.
- **Class of controller:** CBFE is an explicit dual controller in the sense of [mesbah2018stochastic], because the plan does not anticipate learning (q(θ) is fixed within the horizon).
- **Step beyond [kouw2026expected]:** from discrete states and actions to continuous models, with a floor built from the predictive covariance and closed forms for state-space and ARX models.
- **Candidate references** (as todonotes, within the budget of 10–15):
  - Hjalmarsson (2009) or Annergren et al. (2017) for application-oriented design;
  - Heirung, Ydstie and Foss (2017), dual MPC with an ARX model;
  - Toussaint (2009), AICO, for inference-based planning;
  - Russo et al. (2018) for posterior sampling.

**K2. Control reading for the ECC audience**
- **Sec. II:** state the closed-loop criterion of optimal dual control, E Σ_k −ln p_*(y_k) over causal policies, and say that CBFE replaces it by a receding-horizon surrogate.
