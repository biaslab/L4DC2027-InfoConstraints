# A main-text setting with unknown parameters

Written 2026-11-12. The integrator moved to supplementary.tex (Section S2), so the main text needs an experiment with unknown parameters θ, as in the Problem Statement. This note proposes one and reports a pilot.

## Proposal: planar positioning with an unknown actuator orientation

**Problem.** A planar stage, or a robot servoed on image features, moves its measured position through an input frame that is rotated by an unknown angle:

    y_k = y_{k-1} + B u_k + w_k,   w_k ~ N(0, σ² I),   u_k ∈ [-1, 1]²,   B = R(φ),   φ ~ U[0, 2π).

- θ = vec(B), with 4 parameters. The state is measured (g = identity, v = 0).
- From y_0 = 0, the controller reaches a target at distance 0.5 in a random direction, within S_* = 0.01 I, over T = 40 steps.
- The disturbance level is the condition: σ ∈ {0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3}.

**Correspondence to the Problem Statement.**
- f is linear in θ, and the noise is Gaussian.
- The controller knows f, g, the noise family and a prior over θ. The entries of B are independent N(0, ½), which matches the first two moments of a uniform orientation.
- The task is regulation to y_* within S_*, and the metric is the goal cost G per step.

**Model.** All controllers share the ARX model of the integrator and the arm.
- The features are (1, u₁, u₂, y₁, y₂).
- The intercept and the output coefficients are known. The noise level is known to the filter.
- The posterior of B is therefore exact.
- CBFE is the paper's configuration: KKT multiplier, predictive floor, next node, message planner. The floor grid is 39 × 39.

**Why the probing weight must change with σ.**
- With B̂ = 0, every input only adds predictive spread, so the cautious controller never moves. This is the dual effect of an unknown polarity, in two dimensions.
- To reveal B, a probe must stand out from the disturbance. The size of the probe, and hence the probing weight that a dual MPC needs, therefore scales with σ.
- CBFE takes the scale from the floor (1/2) ln|Σ̄ (R + S_*)^{-1}|, so it needs no tuning.

**Physical story.** Options for the paper:
- a stage or mobile base with an unknown mounting angle of its actuators;
- uncalibrated visual servoing, in which the rotation between the camera and the robot frame is unknown.

## Pilot results

Reproduce with `experiment-planar/run.py tune`, `run.py test` and `analysis.py`; the runs take about 4 min on 24 cores. There are 100 test seeds per level, and ρ and the MPC-X accuracy γ are tuned on 30 held-out seeds per level by the mean RMSE over all levels, as in Section 5.

Goal cost per step on the test seeds:

| controller | σ = 0.01 | 0.03 | 0.1 | 0.3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CBFE | 0.62 | 0.71 | 2.02 | 11.9 |
| cautious (γ = 0) | 12.6 | 14.2 | 33.2 | 203 |
| dual MPC, ρ = 30 (tuned over all levels) | 1.79 | 1.88 | 2.88 | 12.0 |
| dual MPC, best ρ per level (1, 3, 10, 30) | 0.54 | 0.75 | 2.12 | 12.0 |
| MPC-X, γ = 0.1 (tuned) | 1.78 | 2.14 | 3.78 | 14.4 |
| oracle (known B) | 0.01 | 0.09 | 1.00 | 8.98 |

**Claims the pilot supports.**
1. **The weight tuned over all levels fails at low disturbance.** The held-out weight ρ = 30 costs 2.9× and 2.7× as much as CBFE at σ = 0.01 and 0.03. The paired differences are +1.17 [1.07, 1.27] and +1.17 [1.09, 1.25].
2. **No fixed weight serves all levels, and normalising the weight does not close the gap.** Every fixed weight costs at least 2.9× as much as CBFE at some level. Weights up to ρ = 10 never move at σ = 0.3 (17×), and ρ ≥ 100 over-probes at σ ≤ 0.03 (7–20×).
   - A reviewer will normalise ρ by the noise-to-tolerance ratio tr(S_*^{-1}R)/n_y = σ²/S_*, the scale that the supplement derives from the leverage (eq. leverage-opt).
   - With ρ = c σ²/S_* and c = 10 selected on the held-out seeds, the dual MPC costs 1.16, 1.11, 1.05 and 1.17× as much as CBFE. The paired differences are +0.100 [0.055, 0.150], +0.080 [0.027, 0.135], +0.095 [0.003, 0.190] and +2.01 [1.52, 2.45], so every interval excludes zero (`analysis.py` prints them).
   - The normalisation removes most of the raw gap but not all of it. The good weight grows like σ (1, 3, 10, 30), whereas σ²/S_* grows like σ², so one constant is too small at σ = 0.01 and too large at σ = 0.3.
   - §5.1 should report this normalised baseline and claim that CBFE beats the best normalised weight, not merely the raw ones.
3. **CBFE matches weights tuned per level.** It stays within 12% of the dual MPC tuned per level. That weight is better by 0.08 [0.04, 0.11] at σ = 0.01, the difference is not significant at σ = 0.03 and 0.3, and CBFE is better by 0.10 [0.00, 0.19] at σ = 0.1.
4. **CBFE beats MPC-X at every level and every accuracy.** MPC-X costs 1.2–3.0× as much as CBFE at γ = 0.1, and γ = 1 does not close the gap. The cause is structural:
   - the requirement of MPC-X ramps with κ(t) over the experiment length, so it waits 1–2 steps at B̂ = 0;
   - it then moves by certainty equivalence with a rotated B̂ and overshoots.
5. **The multiplier switches off and tracks the window of good weights.**
   - The constraint is active on 5–12% of the steps, and is last active at a median step of 2–5.
   - The median active multiplier grows 100× with σ: 0.17, 1.18, 7.6 and 18.3.
   - Twice the median lies inside the window of good weights at every level, as on the integrator and the arm.

**Corroboration.** A 1D pilot on the integrator plant gives the same picture for the dual MPC. It uses b = ±1 with the prior N(0, 1), σ ∈ {0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3} and S_* = 0.01, with 40 seeds and no held-out tuning.
- The best ρ moves from 0.03 to 30.
- Every fixed ρ costs at least 18× as much as CBFE at some σ.
- CBFE is within 27% of the best ρ per level.
- In 1D, however, MPC-X with γ = 0.1 matches CBFE (0.94–1.14×). The advantage of CBFE over MPC-X is therefore specific to the 2D setting and its transient, and the text should not generalise it.

## Risks to check before writing

1. **G does not depend on the scale of S_*.**
   - Multiplying S_* by c divides G by c for every policy, so the optimal controller is the same for a tight and a loose goal.
   - The light–dark numbers show it. The tuned weights satisfy ρ S_* = 1 under both goals (ρ = 100 at 0.01, ρ = 10 at 0.1). The DP attains nearly the same mean squared error under both goals (0.027 and 0.026), whereas CBFE attains 0.036 and 0.048.
   - A reviewer can therefore argue that a weight proportional to S_*^{-1} serves both goals, and that this weight beats CBFE under both.
   - The σ sweep above does not have this weakness, because σ changes the optimal policy.
2. **The parameter floor degenerates for loose goals.**
   - At B̂ = 0, every input has the same mean prediction, so p̃ = p(u) and Σ̄ = σ² + 0.2 per output.
   - CBFE then never probes if S_* ≥ 0.2, i.e., if the goal standard deviation is at least 0.45, about the target distance.
   - The pilot uses S_* = 0.01. A tight-versus-loose sweep on this task must stay below 0.2.
3. **Hidden state plus parameters.**
   - With y = x + v, the joint constraint I[y : x, θ | u] can be met by information about the state alone, so the gain is never probed.
   - The cut light–dark draft split the two layers for this reason.
   - This is why the proposal keeps the state measured.
4. **MPC-X.** Its ramp κ(t) with N = T is the configuration of Larsson et al. and of the integrator. Say so, because a shorter experiment length would make it more urgent.
5. **The same normalisation absorbs the other fixed-weight claims.**
   - Light–dark: the single weight ρ = 1/S_* is the weight tuned per goal, and it beats CBFE under both goals (by 14% and 43%, as main.tex reports).
   - Integrator versus arm: the paper itself explains the gap of two orders of magnitude by tr(S_*^{-1}R_t)/n_y (1 versus 46).
   - The planar σ sweep is the only experiment in which CBFE beats the best normalised weight. It should therefore carry the claim that the multiplier removes the weight, and the other experiments should carry other claims.
