*Simulated ECC 2027 review of the draft of 2026-11-07, 13:36 CET: commit 7988136 plus the uncommitted edits of that day (Sec. V-C and Fig. 4, and the compressed Contributions, Sec. IV-D and Discussion), compiled without todo notes, 7 pages. The reviewer saw only the compiled paper.*

## 1. Summary of the paper

The paper treats receding-horizon control of systems with unknown constant parameters as a constrained variational problem on a Forney-style factor graph of the planning model. Two form constraints (Def. 4.1) fix every belief except the control beliefs to the filtering posteriors and the forward predictions of the model. Under them, the Bethe free energy reduces to a sum of KL input costs and expected negative goal log-densities (Lemma 4.3). An information constraint (Def. 4.2) then requires the conditional mutual information between the parameters and the output of every parameterised node to exceed a floor β_t (Eq. 10). The floor is the entropy by which the parameter-induced predictive covariance at "goal-reaching" inputs exceeds the covariance of the Gaussian goal prior. Stationarity yields a control belief equal to the control prior times a backward message. That message contains the tracking and caution terms of the expected goal log-density plus γ times the information gain (Thm. 4.4, Eqs. 14–15). The KKT multiplier γ is the root of a strictly increasing function: it is zero when the floor is met, and it is truncated at γ_max when the floor is unattainable (Lemma 4.5). The implemented planner is a mode-based, Gauss–Newton/iLQR-type message-passing scheme in which regula falsi solves for one shared multiplier. The paper reports three experiments:

- **Integrator with unknown gain (Åström–Helmersson).** CBFE comes within a few thousandths of RMSE of a tuned dual MPC and of MPC-X, and 14% above a dynamic-programming (DP) solution without input bound.
- **Simulated two-link arm, modelled as ARX, with unknown motor polarity.** CBFE reaches 0.51 rad against 1.59 rad for a dual MPC tuned under known polarity, which applies no torque.
- **Planning cost.** A study compares message passing with gradient descent on the joint input sequence.

## 3. Strengths

- **One derivation gives the dual-control terms.** Lemma 4.3 and Thm. 4.4 yield a control message with exactly the tracking, caution and probing terms of explicit dual control (Eq. 15). The message takes an expected log, whereas belief propagation takes a log of an expectation, so Jensen's inequality explains caution. The special cases γ = 0 (cautious) and γ = 1 (EFE) are identified cleanly.
- **Lemma 4.5 is short and correct.** With the downstream messages fixed, q*(u; γ) is an exponential family in I_b, so h′ = Var[I_b] > 0. This gives a clean three-case characterisation of the multiplier.
- **Lemma 5.1 is correct and useful.** The coefficient information is (n_y/2) ln(1 + leverage), and the noise-precision information does not depend on the input. Eq. (26) explains in a checkable way why EFE never probes when tr(S_*⁻¹R_t) ≥ n_y; I verified the stationary leverage. This insight is useful beyond the paper.
- **The integrator study is carefully set up.** It uses a classical benchmark with a DP reference and 200 episodes per prior mean. All controllers share the model, the filter and the horizon, and the baselines are tuned on held-out seeds. The reported averages match Fig. 3a; I recover 0.118, 0.116, 0.120 and 0.104 from the plot.
- **The body is candid about limitations.** It reports the saturation at γ_max on the arm, the dual MPC at ρ = 2γ_max that matches CBFE, the exponential cost of the input grid and the enforcement at the first step only.
- **The planning cost is linear in the horizon**, with a median of two sweeps. The conditioning argument (the condition number of LᵀL approaches (4K/π)²) is correct; I checked it numerically.

## 4. Weaknesses (most important first)

**W1. The claim that the goal sets a tuning-free probing weight is supported only on the integrator.**
- On the arm, 99.8% of the active multipliers sit at γ_max (Sec. V-B, last paragraph). This is case 3 of Lemma 4.5: the constraint is infeasible, so CBFE becomes a penalty method with the fixed weight γ_max = 50, switched on and off by the sign of β_t.
- Eq. (26) shows that the probing amplitude at saturation is then set directly by γ_max.
- The paper's own dual MPC with ρ = 2γ_max matches CBFE (0.51 rad, 1.39·10³ nats).
- The paper does not say how γ_max = 50 was chosen or how sensitive the arm results are to it. If the results depend on γ_max, then γ_max is the tuned parameter.
- The abstract ("sets this weight from the goal", "without a tuned parameter"), the third contribution bullet (which drops the qualifier "tuned with a known polarity") and the Conclusion ("outperformed them without a tuned parameter") overstate the result.

**W2. The threefold improvement on the arm is measured against a baseline tuned under a different condition than the one tested.**
- ρ is tuned on seeds with known polarity, where tuning selects ρ = 0, i.e., the cautious controller. Under unknown polarity, that controller applies no torque.
- Under known polarity, "all controllers attain 0.49 to 0.51 rad". The tuning criterion is therefore nearly flat in ρ, and choosing ρ = 0 over a probing weight may be a near tie.
- Held-out seeds guard against overfitting to seeds, not against tuning under a different condition.
- The like-for-like baseline is a dual MPC tuned on held-out unknown-polarity seeds. The ρ = 2γ_max result suggests that it would match CBFE.

**W3. The floor (10) is unattainable whenever the goal is tighter than the noise.**
- The floor measures the *total* information needed to shrink the parameter-induced covariance to S_*, whereas (8) is the information of *one* step, relative to the noise.
- Scalar case, with noise variance r, goal variance s and parameter-induced variance v(u): β = ½ ln(v̄/s) and I(u) = ½ ln(1 + v(u)/r), where v̄ = E_p̃[v] ≤ v_max.
  - If s ≥ r, the constraint is always feasible.
  - If s < r, it becomes infeasible as soon as v̄ ≈ v_max and v_max > rs/(r − s). This is the typical situation when the sign of the gain is unknown and the goal-reaching inputs saturate.
- ARX case: Eqs. (23)–(24) give β = (n_y/2) ln ℓ̄* + ½ ln|R_t S_*⁻¹|, with ℓ̄* ≤ max_u ℓ_t. The floor can therefore exceed the attainable information only if |R_t S_*⁻¹| > 1. The 99.8% saturation thus means that the goal (0.014 rad, 0.14 rad/s) is tighter in volume than the one-step noise.
- The integrator uses S_* = σ², exactly the boundary case, where E_p̃[u²] ≤ 1 guarantees feasibility. "Every active multiplier is an interior root" is therefore a consequence of this design choice, not an empirical finding.
- Whether the goal sets the multiplier therefore depends on the ratio of the goal tolerance to the noise, which the paper neither discusses nor tests (sensitivity to S_*).
- The same total demand β_t is imposed on every node of the horizon, although q(θ) is not updated within the plan.

**W4. The implemented planner departs from the analysed problem (Secs. IV-D and VI).**
- (i) The control beliefs are point masses at their modes, which leaves the KL term of Lemma 4.3 undefined. The constraint becomes I_b(u*(γ)) ≥ β. For a global maximiser, this function is nondecreasing in γ but can jump, so the continuity and uniqueness of Lemma 4.5 do not carry over.
- (ii) One multiplier is shared by all nodes but solved from the constraint of the first step. The downstream messages then depend on γ, which contradicts the premise of the proof of Lemma 4.5 ("the messages on the outputs of b do not depend on γ_b"). The result is a KKT point neither of (12) with per-node multipliers nor of an aggregate constraint.
- (iii) Beyond the first step, the predicted states are replaced by their means.
- (iv) In the ARX case, the constraint drops the input-independent part c_t of the information. This is harmless in the message, but it changes the constraint and hence the multiplier.
- Under (6), every node treats θ as independent of its predicted state input. Because the graph with θ has loops, the caution term differs from the exact open-loop caution: for the integrator it is Σ_j P u_j² rather than P(Σ_j u_j)². The claim that γ = 0 gives "the cautious open-loop controller of Section II" therefore holds only under this approximation.

**W5. On the integrator, the statistical evidence is thin, and the benefit is confined to b0 = 0 (Sec. V-A, Fig. 3a).**
- The paper reports no confidence intervals. With the Gaussian goal, (18) gives NLL = E[MSE]/(2σ²) + ½ ln(2πσ²). The reported CBFE pair (0.118, −0.46) then implies a per-episode RMSE standard deviation of about 0.067, so the standard error over 200 episodes is about 0.005 per prior mean. That is the size of the differences between CBFE and the baselines in Fig. 3a.
- The reported averages imply that the cautious controller attains (5·0.179 − 0.436)/4 ≈ 0.115 over b0 ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 4}, the same as CBFE's ≈ 0.114 read from Fig. 3a. This is consistent with a constraint that is active on only 2.1% of the steps.
- On the integrator, the constraint therefore helps only under the symmetric prior. There, CBFE (≈ 0.132) remains about 23% above the DP (≈ 0.107) and above MPC-X (≈ 0.124). The "within 14%" figure averages in the easy cases.
- The DP is computed without the input bound, although a DP over a two-dimensional belief state could easily impose |u| ≤ 1.

**W6. The computational claim (Sec. V-C, "up to 3000 times faster") rests on a weak baseline.**
- First-order gradient descent on an objective whose condition number grows as K² loses to any second-order method, so the result is not specific to message passing.
- The planner is a Gauss–Newton scheme with a Riccati-like backward recursion. O(K) cost per iteration is classical for DDP/iLQR and for Riccati-based Newton steps in MPC.
- The closing argument about a dense K×K factorisation does not hold at these sizes. The Hessian of J is (b̂²/σ²)LᵀL plus a diagonal, which is cheap to form, and its factorisation at K = 100 is negligible. Fair baselines would be L-BFGS, Newton's method on J, or an off-the-shelf iLQR.
- The study uses γ = 1, not CBFE with root finding.

**W7. The positioning and related work need work (Sec. I).**
- Applications-oriented experiment design and MPC-X [14] already derive an information requirement from the control objective and use its dual variable as the probing weight. The paper should state precisely what is new. As far as I can tell, the novelty is tying the tolerance to S_* instead of to a separate accuracy parameter, together with the message-passing derivation.
- Classical dual-control references are missing, e.g., Wittenmark's 1995 survey, Filatov & Unbehauen (2004), Heirung, Ydstie & Foss (2015, 2017), Marafioti, Bitmead & Hovd (2014), Hjalmarsson (2009) and Annergren et al. (2017).
- Inference-based planners close to the proposed message passing are missing, e.g., Toussaint's AICO (2009), Watson et al., "Stochastic optimal control as approximate input inference", and Hoffmann & Rostalski (2017).
- A tuning-free exploration baseline is missing. Posterior (Thompson) sampling also switches exploration off as the posterior concentrates.
- The step beyond the author's own [10] should be made explicit.

**W8. Definitions are missing, which hurts reproducibility.**
- The paper does not give the probing term of the dual MPC or derive the mapping γ = ρ/2. This matters: with ρ = 2 (γ = 1), the dual MPC probes at b0 = 0 (RMSE ≈ 0.152 in Fig. 3a), whereas the γ = 1 (EFE) controller applies no input (0.436). The two objectives therefore cannot coincide.
- The paper does not specify:
  - the control prior p(u), which sets the floor through p̃_t (see Sec. IV-A below);
  - the arm parameters and noise levels;
  - the episode length and the steps per target;
  - the features and the tempering of the prior;
  - the tuning grids;
  - the DP discretisation and horizon.
- No code or supplementary material is mentioned.

**W9. The format needs attention.**
- At 7 pages, the paper is more than half a page over the usual 6-page length. The main text ends on p. 6, and three proofs and the references fill p. 7.
- Two citation keys have no bibliography entry and render as "[?]": iterative LQR (Sec. IV-D) and the variational bound (Sec. VI).

## 5. Detailed comments and questions to the authors

**Abstract, Introduction, Conclusion**
- Please align the claims with Sec. V-B (W1, W2). The third contribution bullet should keep "tuned with a known polarity". The Conclusion's "outperformed them" contradicts the ρ = 2γ_max result.
- The plan does not anticipate learning: q(θ) is fixed within the horizon, and Sec. IV-A states that "the plan is open loop". CBFE is thus an explicit dual controller in the sense of [3], and saying so early would help readers.

**Sec. II**
- Please state the closed-loop criterion that the "optimal dual controller" optimises, and relate the CBFE objective to it.
- The factorised filtering prior p(x_t|D_t)·p(θ|D_t) of Sec. III is exact only when the state is measured, as in both examples. Please qualify the claim of generality for state-space models.

**Sec. III**
- Please state that θ is shared across slices through equality nodes, which creates loops. Please also discuss how (6) discards the correlation between θ and the predicted states (W4).

**Sec. IV-A**
- The floor depends on p(u) through p̃_t. When b̂_t = 0, p̃_t = p(u) and β_t = ½ ln(P_t E_p[u²]/σ²), which the control prior sets rather than the goal. The constraint is active mostly at the first step for b0 ≤ 0.5, so the b0 = 0 results depend on p(u). Please specify p(u) and show the sensitivity to it.
- The floor (10) takes the log-determinant of E[Σ^θ]. If the parameters do not reach every output within one step, β_t = −∞ and the constraint never activates. An example is an Euler-discretised mechanical system with an unknown input gain and position–velocity outputs. How would you handle a rank-deficient Σ^θ?
- What is the rationale for imposing the same β_t on every node of the horizon (W3)? Please also explain "the goal-reaching inputs p̃_t omit the caution of Section II".

**Sec. IV-B**
- Lemma 4.3 is fine. Please state that the factors are normalised conditionals, and that the half-edges at the end of the horizon (the last state and parameter copies in Fig. 2a) are excluded from the sum of edge entropies in (4).
- Thm. 4.4: the proof needs every edge belief to be multilinear in the control beliefs, so that 𝓛 is a KL divergence plus a linear term in each q(u_k). This is where q(θ) fixed and a tree-shaped graph without θ enter. Please state these assumptions in the theorem; at present the tree assumption appears only in Sec. IV-D.
- q* is a Gibbs policy whose mode is applied. In control terms, CBFE is an MPC with the stage cost −ln p(u_k) − E[ln p_*(y_k)] − γ I_{a_k}(u_k) and a time-varying γ set by a rule. Saying so would make the contribution clearer to the ECC audience.

**Sec. IV-C**
- Lemma 4.5 needs ∫ p μ₀ e^{γ I_b} du < ∞ for all γ, and μ₀ > 0 p-almost everywhere for mutual absolute continuity. Both hold on a compact 𝒰, but not in general.
- The truncation at γ_max in case 3 is an algorithmic choice; please move it out of the lemma.
- With mode beliefs (W4-i), does regula falsi ever stop at a discontinuity of h? How many replans does the root finding take per step?

**Sec. IV-D**
- The information term is nonconvex in u; for the integrator, I″ < 0 when P u² > σ². How do the Gaussian backward messages handle it? Is the negative curvature dropped?
- Is "also starts from the constant plan at ±0.5" a multistart with selection by J? For n_u = 2, are all sign combinations tried?
- What grid size is used for the floor?

**Sec. V (setup)**
- Please give the dual MPC cost and derive γ = ρ/2. Please also explain the discrepancy between ρ = 2 and EFE (W8).
- Please define the "accuracy" of MPC-X. An MPC-X variant whose accuracy is set from S_* would separate the effect of the Bethe formulation from the choice of tolerance.
- Please report the tuning grids, the number of held-out seeds and the tuning criterion.
- Consider certainty-equivalence MPC and posterior-sampling MPC as additional baselines.

**Sec. V-A**
- Please give per-b0 results with confidence intervals for the paired differences, since the seeds are common to all controllers. Please also add the cautious and EFE curves to Fig. 3a.
- Please compute the DP with |u| ≤ 1. Note also that the DP minimises E Σ y², not the episode-averaged RMSE.
- Please test the sensitivity to S_* (e.g., σ²/4 and 4σ²). By W3, S_* < σ² should drive the integrator into saturation.
- "within 0.006 of them for b0 ≥ 0.5": in my reading of the log-scaled Fig. 3a, CBFE attains ≈ 0.131 and the dual MPC ≈ 0.124 at b0 = 0.5, a gap of about 0.007. Please check.
- A plot of u_t and P_t for one b0 = 0 episode, next to the DP solution, would show what the single probing step does.

**Sec. V-B**
- Please report the arm RMSE of CBFE for a range of γ_max (e.g., 5–500), and the ρ-tuning curve under both polarity conditions.
- Please add the dual MPC at ρ = 2γ_max, the cautious controller and EFE to Fig. 3b. Please report medians and interquartile ranges or per-seed results, because one seed at 6.75 rad dominates the MPC-X mean.
- An RMSE of ≈ 0.5 rad for every controller under known polarity is about 35 goal standard deviations, so transients after the target switches dominate the metric. Settling time, final error per target and input energy would show what switching probing off buys. At present, a dual MPC that probes at a constant weight matches CBFE, so that benefit is not demonstrated.
- Please justify dropping c_t from the constraint, for example by its consistency with the coefficient-only floor (24).
- Please give the experimental details:
  - the link parameters and the process and measurement noise;
  - dim φ, the features and the tempering;
  - the variance of the zero-mean sign prior;
  - the episode length, steps per target, target locations and the inverse-kinematics branch.

  Are the joint angles wrapped? 6.75 rad exceeds one revolution.
- In what fraction of the seeds is the polarity identified after round 1? Please break the results down by the number of reversed motors.
- 63–89 ms per step against a period of 100 ms leaves little margin. Please split this time into planning, root finding and floor evaluation.

**Sec. V-C**
- See W6 for the baselines. Please also benchmark the full CBFE, with the multiplier and the floor grid.
- The stated speed-ups (3.2× at K = 2, 3000× at K = 100) differ from the ratios of the reported medians (1.5/0.41 ≈ 3.7; 56 s/16 ms ≈ 3500). Are they medians of per-problem ratios?

**Sec. VI**
- Please discuss, at least qualitatively, closed-loop stability, the consistency of the parameter estimates after probing stops, and model mismatch, since the ARX model with Fourier features only approximates the arm dynamics.
- Does the convex dual problem of [10] carry over to the continuous case?

**Appendix**
- Proof of Thm. 4.4: please write out the induction from the goal nodes back to u_k.
- Proof of Lemma 4.5: please add the integrability condition.
- Proof of Lemma 5.1: y_k must be centred by M_tᵀφ_k before it is scaled by (1 + ℓ_t)^{-1/2}.

## 7. Confidential comments to the programme committee

The formulation is elegant, and the stated lemmas and theorem appear correct. However, the headline claims rest on comparisons that are not like-for-like:
- on the arm, the multiplier saturates at a fixed γ_max;
- the "threefold" gain is measured against a baseline tuned under a different condition;
- the 3000× speed-up is measured against first-order gradient descent.

The paper is also more than half a page over the usual length, and two references are missing. I would support acceptance only if the claims are recalibrated and the authors add a γ_max sensitivity study and a like-for-like tuned baseline. As it stands, I rate it borderline.
